# Global Swipe — Open/Close Drawer from Anywhere (touch-slop axis decision)

*Established: 2026-06-05; reworked 2026-06-10 to a UIKit exclusive pan; **reworked again 2026-06-10** to the touch-slop + failure-requirement pattern at the user's direction.*

- The drawer swipe works from **anywhere** on screen, not just the left edge. User verbatim: *"i want swipe open and close the hidden left panel."*
- **Swipe right** (when drawer is closed) opens it; **swipe left** (when open) closes it. On release, it snaps open/closed by **projected end position** (translation + ~150 ms of release velocity — a quick flick counts).
- **One axis per touch — decided up front by touch slop, enforced by a UIKit failure requirement.** User 2026-06-06/07: *"i told you to disable the scrolling horizentally and vertically together"* / *"now the app can do both at the same time, which is not desired."* User 2026-06-10, rejecting the first (exclusivity-only) rework: *"thats not right, i am thiking about adding a small delay of the vertical scrolling, and decide if the scrolling is veritical or horizontal"* — confirmed by research as the platform-canonical pattern (sliding-drawer libraries, Apple failure requirements). The "delay" is a **movement budget (~10 pt of finger travel, "touch slop"), not a timer.** Implementation is **`DrawerPanGestureModifier`** (in [Nutritionist/ContentView.swift](Nutritionist/ContentView.swift)) — a `UIGestureRecognizerRepresentable` on the root ZStack wrapping **`DrawerAxisPanGestureRecognizer`** (a `UIPanGestureRecognizer` subclass):
  - **Slop decision (the subclass):** the touch accumulates `axisSlop = 10` pt of movement (tracked in window coords), then decides ONCE from the **accumulated translation** — never the instantaneous velocity, which is noisy at touch-start and misclassified slow/diagonal swipes (the rejected velocity gate). Horizontal-dominant AND (open OR rightward) → it begins; otherwise → `state = .failed` immediately. `reset()` clears the decision per touch.
  - **`shouldBeRequiredToFailBy` → `true` for every `UIScrollView.panGestureRecognizer`** (the load-bearing line): scroll pans must WAIT for the drawer pan to fail before they may begin. A vertical touch fails the drawer within the slop and scrolls as normal (the user's "small delay"); a horizontal touch begins the drawer and the scroll never starts at all. This kills the race the exclusivity-only version had — a vertical ScrollView's pan recognizes movement in ANY direction, so on a horizontal swipe it usually began first and force-failed the drawer.
  - **Horizontal rails** (pinned-chip strip, AttachTray photo rail): a touch starting inside a `UIScrollView` whose `contentSize.width` overflows its bounds **insta-fails the drawer pan in `touchesBegan`**, so the rail's own pan starts with zero added delay. (This replaced the old `shouldReceive` carve-out — the recognizer must *receive and fail* rather than not receive, so the failure requirement always resolves.)
  - `shouldRecognizeSimultaneouslyWith` → **`false`** stays as belt-and-braces exclusivity; `gestureRecognizerShouldBegin` consults the slop decision (falling back to a translation-dominance check if the pan's internal threshold trips first); keeps the default `cancelsTouchesInView = true`; `maximumNumberOfTouches = 1`.
- `drawerPanChanged` / `drawerPanEnded` in `ContentView` consume the callbacks (`isDrawerDragging` + `drawerDrag` drive `drawerOffset`/`drawerProgress` as before); `allowsHitTesting(!isDrawerDragging)` on the content layer stays as a belt-and-braces freeze.
- **iOS 17/18 fallback** (no `UIGestureRecognizerRepresentable` there): the old cooperative `simultaneousGesture` `DragGesture(minimumDistance: 14, coordinateSpace: .global)` with horizontal-dominance + axis-latch, kept inside `DrawerPanGestureModifier`'s `else` branch. Don't "simplify" the modifier down to one path.
- **Don't** decide the axis from instantaneous velocity (rejected 2026-06-10 — *"thats not right"*); don't drop the `shouldBeRequiredToFailBy` requirement (re-introduces the scroll-begins-first race); don't swap `shouldRecognizeSimultaneouslyWith` to `true` (both-axes-at-once); don't replace the UIKit path with `highPriorityGesture` (steals taps); don't turn the slop into a wall-clock timer (it's a distance); don't remove the horizontal-rail insta-fail in `touchesBegan` (kills the chip strip / photo rail) and don't convert it back to a `shouldReceive` refusal (an unreceived touch can leave the failure requirement unresolved).
- **Today food-log card (2026-06-11 fix — drawer dead zone).** User: *"why when i swipe to right on the main screen from the food log it doesnt respond?? the swiping right should be a global gesture."* The card's vertical scroll-drag was a `.highPriorityGesture(DragGesture(minimumDistance: 10))` that CLAIMED every touch after 10 pt regardless of direction, then ignored horizontal ones (date swipe is hard-disabled) — force-failing the drawer pan. On iOS 26 the card drag is now **`TodayCardPanModifier`** (in [Nutritionist/Views/TodayView.swift](Nutritionist/Views/TodayView.swift)) wrapping **`TodayCardAxisPanGestureRecognizer`** — the mirror image of the drawer recognizer: slop decision, **vertical → begins** (feeds `homeCardPanChanged`/`homeCardPanEnded`, same rubber-band/commit math), **horizontal → fails immediately**, releasing the touch to the drawer. The SwiftUI `homeHorizontalCardSwipeGesture` remains ONLY as the iOS 17/18 fallback. **Don't reattach the SwiftUI drag via `highPriorityGesture` on iOS 26** — any gesture on the card that claims horizontal touches re-creates the dead zone.
